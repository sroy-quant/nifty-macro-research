Can macro data predict NIFTY 50 direction?

Overnight S&P 500, VIX, US yields, the dollar and crude are often sold as a "99% accurate" Nifty predictor. I tested it on 11 years of data with frozen rules, an untouched test period and real trading costs. Every chart here recalculates live from the daily data.

✓ Predicts the opening gapData known before 09:15 explains about 26% of the gap.
✗ Not the tradable moveOpen-to-close calls were right less often than always calling "up" in the untouched test, and lost money after costs.
✓ The regime signal holdsFear regimes were followed by stronger 20-day returns in every period.

The regime signal

Fear means India VIX is in its top 20% of the past year, or Nifty is at least 8% below its 52-week high; both together is "fear (both)". Mid-zone means 2–8% below the high with calm VIX. Both are known before the open.

Method & lessons

The same discipline used in production analytics: define the decision, freeze the logic, then measure against a baseline on data the model has never seen.

01 · TIMING

Known before 09:15?

Every driver is tagged by when it is known, and only before-open data can predict.

02 · FREEZE

Fix the rules first

Weights, signs and thresholds were chosen on 2015–19 only, then frozen.

03 · TEST

Untouched data

2020–24 was used once and compared with the base rate, year by year, and with a shuffled benchmark.

04 · COSTS

After real costs

Brokerage, taxes and slippage are included, and the failures are reported too.

Lessons

  1. Most "predictive" macro links are same-day explanations. Split drivers by when they are known.
  2. Overnight data is priced into the opening gap. The tradable move is close to a coin flip.
  3. Slow regimes (fear vs mid-zone) carry more usable information than daily direction.
  4. Beat the base rate, not 50%, year by year, after costs.
  5. The popular "monthly expiry days fall" pattern (71% down in 2025–26) was 47–49% in 2015–24, a small-sample artifact.
